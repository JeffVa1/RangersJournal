# The Shattered Crown - Part 1 Recaps

## Session 1 - 8/27/25

### The Dragon’s Breath Tavern

Our adventurers began their tale in the Dragon’s Breath tavern, nestled deep in the slums of Drakensport. As they went about their business, a heated argument erupted among the patrons. Hammond, a familiar face in these parts, stepped forward to intervene. Instead of restoring peace, however, he launched into a fiery speech, calling on everyone to rise against the tyranny of the God-King. His defiance chilled the room, and one by one, the tavern’s customers slipped away, fearful of the inquisitors’ wrath.

### Hammond’s Secret Mission

While most fled, Ace, Elyndor, and the Ranger lingered, intrigued by Hammond’s words. At his urging, they followed him to a back room. There, he presented them with traveling gear, weapons, a backpack, a note, and a map. The note carried a charge: recover the fabled Lost Scrolls of Irillis. On the map, a hidden smuggler’s trail was marked, leading to the entrance of the ancient crypts.

### The Marshals’ Arrival

As the group prepared to leave, Kaelthar conjured a solid bar of ice across the tavern door to delay pursuit. Moments later, heavy pounding shook the wood, followed by the stern voices of Marshals demanding entry. The tavern keeper hurried his young barhand, Meek, into the kitchens in a desperate attempt to keep him safe.

But as the party made their way toward the exit, Ace could not bear to abandon Meek. He turned back just as the Marshals broke through. Panicked, Meek bolted straight toward them—and was cut down in an instant, a blade driven into his chest.

### Flight from Drakensport

Fury overtook Ace, and he hurled himself at the Marshals, with Hammond and Elyndor at his side. Though Hammond fought to buy them time, the arrival of an Inquisitor forced the party to retreat. Their escape was narrow, and as they fled into the night, the sounds of Hammond’s brutal beating echoed behind them.

### Into the Wilds

Leaving the Dragon’s Breath, the companions followed the smuggler’s trail out of the city. In the forest beyond, they came across a hulking troll prowling near the path. Just as they began to creep past, Elyndor called upon his Healing Light—a flash that nearly drew the creature’s attention. Thinking quickly, Kaelthar conjured a Minor Illusion, sending a phantom sound deeper into the woods. Distracted, the troll lumbered toward the noise, allowing the party to slip away unseen.

Soon after, they looked for a safe place to make camp.

### Nightfall

As the firelight flickered, Elyndor forged a bond with his pact weapon, while Ace and Kaelthar passed the time with a game of Liar’s Die—Kaelthar securing victory through a bit of clever cheating.

Their first night on the road passed without further disturbance, the quiet woods hiding what adventures lay ahead.

Our party has made it a little over half way to the Hidden Cavern.

## Session 2 - 9/3/25

### A Vision of Light

As dawn broke, Elyndor awoke to a blinding radiance. A voice spoke within the light, solemn and commanding:

"Child of light, you walk the path that few dare tread. The God-King’s shadow grows long, yet you burn with the spark to stand against him. The Lost Scrolls lie ahead—fragments of a truth buried in fire and ruin. Take them, claim them, guard them well. Others will seek them, but they were meant for you.

Do not falter, Elyndor. Trust in me, as I trust in you. Every trial will shape you closer to what you must become. With the scrolls, the chains of this world can be broken, and a new dawn shall rise. When the way is hidden, remember: I will guide your steps. Together, we shall bring about the age that was promised."

When Elyndor’s vision faded, the light was gone, leaving only the weight of destiny pressing heavily upon him. Only Ace noticed that Elyndor was acting strangely, but he did not inquire, and Elyndor did not volunteer an explanation.

### The Goblin Camp

The companions pressed on, keeping to the cover of the woods rather than the open smuggler’s trail. Ranger soon picked up signs of a goblin band and tracked them to their camp. He scouted quietly, relaying what he saw back to the others with practiced hand signs.

Striking with surprise, the party descended upon five goblins and their boss. The skirmish was swift and decisive. One goblin attempted to flee, but Ace cut him down—severing both legs before trying, in vain, to interrogate him. The goblin bled out before a word could be spoken. The group looted the camp, finding a modest pouch of gold.

### The Crypt’s Door

Following the trail once more, the adventurers pressed deeper into the forest until, at dusk, they emerged into a clearing. Before them stood three statues and a great stone door carved with three interlocking circles—the ancient symbol of Irillis. In Elvish script, the door bore the words: “The broken cannot.”

Each statue bore a single word: See. Speak. Spar.

The party experimented, first offering oil, water, and blood—each sacrifice sizzling away into nothing. Then, Ranger spoke in Elvish at the “Speak” altar, which began to glow. At the “Spar” altar, Evander sliced Ranger’s hand, letting a drop of blood fall, and it too shone with light. Finally, Elyndor cast Light upon the “See” altar, completing the ritual. All three statues glowed, and the great stone door rumbled open, sliding into the earth to reveal the Crypt of Irillis.

### The Undead Horde

Inside, the crypt was far from silent. The first steps into its halls awakened skeletons and zombies, their shambling forms converging upon the intruders. The party fought fiercely, felling the undead with little harm—until Elyndor triggered a hidden trap, a poisoned arrow piercing his side.

As the fighting subsided, the adventurers began to explore the main chamber. Kaelthar, ever cautious, cast Detect Magic and sensed a faint magical glow emanating from the ceiling above. Meanwhile, Ace, ever bold, strayed ahead and stumbled into another pack of undead, beyond which a chest glimmered faintly in the torchlight. The rest of the party rushed to his aid, cutting down the monsters and clearing the chamber.

### The Chest of Irillis

Cautiously, Kaelthar inspected the chest, noting both a vent and a tripwire—clear signs of a trap. With deft skill, Ranger disarmed and unlocked it, revealing treasures within:

A dagger of sharp enchantment (+2), claimed by Elyndor.

A longbow of deadly craft (+1), taken by Ranger.

Two healing potions, given to Evander and Kaelthar.

And a small cache of coin: 3 copper, 4 silver, and 17 gold, divided among the party.

### A Hard-Earned Rest

The group secured the chamber for the night. Ranger set an Alarm to guard their sleep, and at last, the adventurers rested, safe for now within the crypt’s stone embrace.

## Session 3 - 9/8/25

### Whispers of the Dead

The adventurers awoke within the crypt, still in the chamber where they had spilled the blood of zombies and skeletons the night before. The silence was heavy, broken only by the echo of dripping water and the weight of unseen eyes. Returning to the main hall, they sought answers.

Kaelthar turned his mind once more to the faint magical essence lingering in the ceiling but could not discern its purpose. The statue within the chamber offered little more; even with Ranger’s help, he learned only that it held meaning to the Irillis people. Ranger’s gaze turned to the inscription on the floor, and he pieced together its symbolism—an ancient Irillis custom honoring their fallen and giving thanks for sacrifices long buried in the stone.

### Traps of Flame

With their investigation concluded, the party pressed eastward, climbing the stairs to the upper hall. The corridor stretched long, broken by a single doorway midway. As they advanced, Kaelthar noticed a stone statue faintly infused with fading magic—an old trap, long defunct. Yet danger still lurked.

A pressure plate clicked beneath Ranger, Ace, and Evander. This trap was alive with magic. Acting quickly, Kaelthar froze the water from his own pack, sealing the edges of the plate with ice. He shouted for his companions to step back, and only when they were safe did he reclaim the water. The statue roared to life, spewing fire across the corridor. The way forward was barred.

The party retreated and tried the western stairs. Again, Kaelthar marked the husk of an old statue trap, and again, a matching active one ahead. Two pressure plates lay in wait before it. Ace ran first, leaping past both with reckless confidence, and called for the others to follow. One by one, they crossed safely.

### The Knife of Truth

Beyond the traps, they entered a chamber shrouded in foreboding. Four statues stood in the corners, each holding bowls. At the center loomed a grim reaper carved of stone, scythe in hand. Before him, an altar bore a curved dagger with a red handle, glowing faintly with purple light. Inscribed upon the stone: “The blood of truth reveals all.”

The corner statues each carried their own grim message:

Knowledge is forbidden

God-King never dies

Obedience is life

All chains must break

Ace seized the dagger and cut his hand, letting his blood fall into the bowl marked “All chains must break.” The bowl and blade disintegrated into purple light, which rose and twisted into the form of the Lost Scrolls of Irillis. The glow then shot southward, through the crypt wall, and the ground rumbled with the grinding of stone.

### The Ogre’s Awakening

The adventurers rushed back to the main chamber—only to find a nightmare waiting. A hulking ogre zombie stood at its center, eyes burning with violet light, flanked by three lesser zombies. As the party entered, jagged purple crystals erupted from the floor, sealing every exit.

Ranger struck first, ensnaring the beast with thorned vines while his companions unleashed blow after blow. But with a bellowing roar, the ogre’s wounds closed, and across the crypt the corpses of slain undead stirred once more. Skeletons and zombies clawed their way back to life, hammering against the crystal walls.

The battle raged. The adventurers cut down wave after wave, but each time the host with violet eyes fell, the purple glow leapt into another corpse, driving it forward with unholy strength. At last, Ace struck the final blow against the ogre—but the cursed light leapt from its skull into a skeleton beyond. The crystal barriers shattered, and the horde flooded in.

Hemmed in, battered, and bloodied, the party made their stand. One by one, they destroyed the undead vessels until, finally, the purple glow abandoned flesh and flew into the statue of a woman at the chamber’s edge. Her stone mouth moved, and her voice echoed in the dark:

"The trials have been passed.

Heirs of ruin… you have broken the silence.

You have been deemed worthy. The seal of Irillis is opened in the shadow of Skyfang.

Know this: the God-King will sense what you have done. His gaze is unblinking, his reach unending. He will loose his hounds of shadow,and they will hunt you without rest, without mercy.

Yet take heart. If his servants pursue you, it is because you carry the hope he fears. Guard it well, for through you the chains may yet be broken.

Go swiftly. Go wisely. Irillis walks with you."

### Escape from the Crypt

In that moment, the adventurers realized the faint glow Kaelthar had sensed in the ceiling was the hidden chamber that held the ogre all along. As the statue’s words faded, the final crystal wall dissolved, revealing a way out.

The party staggered from the crypt, scarred but victorious. They took a short rest outside, catching their breath under the open sky. Leaving the ruins behind, they traveled for an hour along the coast, keeping parallel to the smuggler’s trail.

On the shore, Ranger, Kaelthar, and Evander fished while Ace hunted rabbits. As night fell, Kaelthar and Ace passed the time with another game of Liar’s Die. Once again, Kaelthar proved the better, walking away with more of Ace’s gold.

Thus ended the third chapter of their journey—haunted by the God-King’s gaze, yet carrying the fragile hope of Irillis into the dark beyond.

## Session 4 - 9/17/25

### The Shattered Caravan

The companions pressed onward along the winding Smugglers’ Road, the forest falling eerily silent around them. Before long, they came upon the splintered wreckage of a caravan—broken crates, blood-stained earth, and the stench of death thick in the air. No survivors remained, only the echoes of slaughter.

Amid the ruin, a torn pack was found, and within it, a letter: a bounty for a slave named Gerald Smallwood, missing a finger from his left hand, to be delivered to Brightmarch for fifteen gold. The note spoke of secrecy, of betrayal, and of meeting at the White Mare Inn with a password known as “the God-King’s special.”

As the party studied the carnage, the forest erupted. Bugbears burst from the treeline with snarling wolves at their sides. Steel and claws clashed in the blood-soaked mud. Arrows flew, spells struck, and blades tore through fur and flesh. Ace cut a bugbear clean in half, while Kaelthar mocked him and Evander alike with the title “Bugbear Butchers of Brightmarch.” When the last foe fell, their laughter turned to rivalry—an arm-wrestling match of grit and pride. Ace won, slamming Evander’s arm to the ground in triumph.

### Into the Sewers

By nightfall, the companions reached Brightmarch’s edge, slipping toward a rusted sewer gate. A guard’s voice rang out, but the party scattered, diving into the city’s underbelly before pursuit could tighten. The stench of rot and filth closed around them, rats swarming in waves until Kaelthar incinerated the horde with a blast of chromatic power.

Deeper in, they discovered a locked chamber. Inside lay the bones of a long-dead traveler, slumped beside several rotting chests. Yet Kaelthar’s keen eyes saw through illusion. One chest twisted and screeched, revealing itself as a mimic. Its sticky tongue lashed out, dragging Ranger forward before Kaelthar’s spell broke its hold. The beast was relentless, slamming Evander to the ground, but the party rallied—steel, spells, and arrows tearing it apart. When it fell, only mold and stale rations remained.

The tunnels wound deeper. A swarm of insects crawled from the cracks, covering Ranger in writhing filth. Beyond, a statue of the God-King loomed. Evander struck it with defiance, and the stone split in two.

### The Control Room

Around the bend, the party entered a strange chamber bristling with pipes and levers—a control room for the sewer’s water system. At its center oozed a massive gelatinous cube, its translucent body brimming with half-dissolved bones and weapons.

The fight was desperate. The ooze lashed out, trying to engulf Elyndor and Evander, nearly consuming them. Kaelthar’s magic splintered its form, and Elyndor carved down the fragments. All the while, Ace pulled levers—shutting off the water flow into the main chamber, then halting the city’s water supply entirely. But even as the cube died, its liquefied corpse was sucked through the pipes, carried upward into Brightmarch’s water system before the final lever was thrown.

### Rest in the Depths

Exhausted and bloodied, the adventurers did not yet leave the sewers. Instead, they pressed into a side chamber that seemed once to have served as a resting place for workers—a break room buried in filth and silence. There, they laid down their arms and took their rest, the foul air of the tunnels clinging to their skin.

### Wind Walker

That night, Ranger’s bond with his longbow deepened. Wind Walker was no longer a relic—it had become a partner, its power awakening to match his own.

## Session 5 - 10/15/25

### Surrounded Below

In the damp worker’s chamber beneath Brightmarch, the party’s uneasy rest was broken by a whisper from beyond. Elyndor’s celestial patron spoke within his mind:“Beware… you are surrounded.”

Moments later, the sound of armored boots echoed through the tunnels. Ace crept to the doorway and saw them—five Marshals closing in through the dark. Ranger raised the alarm, but too late. The trap had already been sprung.

Ace slammed the main door shut as Kaelthar froze it solid, sealing it tight. Three Marshals were locked out, their fists pounding against the icy barrier, while two more pushed in from the opposite passage.

The cramped tunnel erupted into chaos. Elyndor took a heavy blow but healed himself with divine light. Evander struck back with fierce resolve. Ace unleashed his wild magic in a burst of unpredictable flame, while Kaelthar’s silver draconic breath scorched the advancing soldiers.

Ranger’s arrows struck true, dropping one of the attackers, and Elyndor’s blade claimed another. The final Marshal was taken alive—barely—after Elyndor’s sword carved deeper than intended. Kaelthar scowled at the recklessness.

The group hid the bodies behind a statue in the nearby prayer room, the scent of blood thick in the air. When the captured Marshal awoke, the party interrogated him, but he offered little—he had followed them out of suspicion, not by order. Once his usefulness had ended, Kaelthar ended his life quietly.

### The Streets of Brightmarch

Further exploration of the tunnels revealed strange knobs embedded in the stone walls. Turning them caused sewage to surge into the chamber before the party quickly reversed their mistake. Eventually, they found a way out. Ace tore through the last sewer grate, and sunlight spilled down through the muck and shadow.

They emerged into the holy naval city of Brightmarch—a place of faith and salt air, where cathedral spires gleam above bustling harbors and narrow canals thread through the northern quarters. Incense, brine, and the scent of tar mixed on the wind.

A halfling fisherman stood nearby on the canal’s edge, humming tunelessly to himself. He called himself Biigs. Ace greeted him warmly, but Biigs ignored him, even eating the gold he was offered. He cast his line and drew forth a silvery, metallic duck with swirling runic patterns—only for it to dissolve upon touching the water. Laughing, he resumed his eerie tune, denying any knowledge of Hammond.

### Whispers in the Market

The party moved toward the western docks, where the markets bustled with fear and rumor. Merchants whispered of a child named Lucy gone missing—only a pool of blood left behind. Evander overheard frantic guards muttering that people were melting after drinking the city’s water.

Supplies were bought, bartered, or stolen as the adventurers prepared to investigate further.

### The Golden Gallon

At last, the companions arrived at the Golden Gallon tavern, keeping their distance to avoid attention. Kaelthar’s magical senses pricked with unease. A faint arcane pulse grew stronger as a familiar figure entered—Hammond.

But Hammond’s demeanor was wrong. When he demanded the Lost Scrolls, Kaelthar’s eyes briefly flared with violet light—a subconscious flicker of obedience rooted in his dragonborn bloodline. Only Ranger noticed the flash.

Hammond’s voice hardened. In an instant, the illusion shattered—his form melting away to reveal an Inquisitor of the God-King. Another illusion broke as well: a drunken patron at the bar dissolved into a dragonborn mage. The trap was sprung.

The Inquisitor cast Hold Person on Ranger and wrenched the scrolls free. The dragonborn mage vanished in a shattering mist, disappearing outside. The Inquisitor followed, Misty Stepping into the street where he summoned his vrock—a winged horror that screamed across the rooftops.

The party gave chase. Kaelthar hurled frost toward one of the vrock’s wings while Ranger’s Ensnaring Strike caught the other, but the creature managed to stay aloft. Evander, with divine command, shouted a single word—“Drop!”—and the Inquisitor obeyed, letting the scrolls tumble to the ground.

As the vrock swooped to retrieve them, Ace launched from the tavern roof, his blade a blur of fury. He struck deep into the creature’s wings, sending it crashing into the street below.

Inside the tavern, Elyndor blasted the locked door apart to escape with the scrolls—the bartender behind it was torn apart by splintered wood, dying instantly. The Inquisitor followed him in but lost sight of his quarry as Elyndor slipped into a side room, hiding the true scrolls and conjuring an illusory duplicate. He emerged with the false copy in hand, baiting his pursuer.

The Inquisitor, blinded by zeal, ignored the other room and followed Elyndor through the bar. Outside, the others fought the vrock, keeping it prone and wounded. Ranger’s arrow found its mark, piercing the beast’s throat. Inside, Ace charged through the broken door to join Elyndor.

The Inquisitor struck a near-fatal blow, leaving Elyndor bloodied—but not broken. With the last of his strength, Elyndor delivered his vengeance, cutting the Inquisitor apart in a storm of a thousand shallow wounds.

### The Bells of Brightmarch

As the final echoes of battle faded, the Golden Gallon lay in ruins—splintered beams, blood, and broken glass. The vrock lay dead outside, the Inquisitor’s body torn to ribbons within. The dragonborn mage had vanished into the chaos.

Then came the tolling of alarm bells, spreading across Brightmarch like a plague of sound.

The city had awoken to the slaughter—and the hunt had begun.

## Session 6 - 11/3/25

### The Hunt Begins - Interlude

### Recap

### Ashes of the Golden Gallon

The tavern was quiet now, save for the crackle of dying flames and the distant tolling of alarm bells. The inquisitor’s body lay twisted among the wreckage, his golden mask gleaming faintly in the firelight. Kaelthar and Ranger knelt beside him, studying the corpse.

The armor was unlike anything forged in Brightmarch—metal that seemed to hum softly, its etched runes shifting when viewed from the corner of the eye. Kaelthar carefully removed the mask, revealing a face blackened and burned in the very shape of its engravings. Where eyes should have been, there were only smooth sockets—empty, inhuman. On his ears hung dark, glassy earrings as cold as night. Through Kaelthars careful examination, the group discovered that the masks were mundane, non magical. The earrings however, hummed with an intense and dark magic. Evander decided to throw the body, and earrings into the fire.

Ranger pocketed the mask as a trophy. Together they examined the armor—enchanted plate of extraordinary craftsmanship, marked by faint traces of divine corruption.

Meanwhile, Ace prowled through the ruined tavern, turning over splintered tables and broken bottles until he uncovered a small box of coins. He swore there must be more and continued the search.

### The Hidden Cellar

In the corner of the bar, Ranger found a trapdoor leading into darkness. He and Kaelthar descended, discovering a locked chest filled with strange trinkets and arcane components. The air shimmered faintly—charged with magic.

Then, from the gloom, a voice spoke. A tall elf emerged, half-invisible and cloaked in shifting light. He questioned them sharply about the inquisitor, about Hammond, and about what had transpired above. Before they could reply, he urged them to buy him time—he would open a way out.

They returned upstairs. The party braced the doors, overturned tables, and doused the floors with oil. Outside, the shouts of Marshals grew louder. One smashed through the barricade just as Ranger’s flint sparked to life, igniting the oil in a rush of flame. The soldier was consumed in the blaze.

Ranger shouted for the others to move—down through the trapdoor.

### The Professor’s Refuge

Below, the mysterious elf stood within a glowing blue rune. “Inside the circle!” he commanded. The party obeyed. The world folded in on itself, and darkness swallowed them whole.

When their vision cleared, they stood in a dim laboratory before a startled blue dragonborn. He wore soot-stained robes and spoke with bitter disdain for Brightmarch and the corruption festering within it. He introduced himself as Professor Elric Vaunt—a scholar of forbidden arcana and one of the resistance’s hidden leaders.

According to Vaunt, the God-King’s magic twisted dragonborn bloodlines, warping them into vessels of obedience. He asked for the party’s help retrieving his pack from across the street, where the infected now wandered the canals. Ace volunteered, but Ranger demanded collateral—the gold Ace had found earlier. With a reluctant sigh, Ace handed it over, then returned moments later with the professor’s belongings intact.

Ranger pressed Vaunt for a way to cure Brightmarch’s corruption. The dragonborn only shook his head. “The rot runs too deep,” he murmured.

On a nearby desk, Ranger found parchment filled with arcane scrawl—notes detailing the inquisitors’ masks:

### On the Golden Masks of the Inquisitor

### Professor Elric Vaunt

Every inquisitor wears as mask of purest gold. No eyes. No mouth. No openings of any kind. Yet they move as though they see and hear perfectly.

They do not speak. Never. Not to one another, not to the enslaved. Silence absolute. Commands are given, yet no word is spoken.

The thralls - The God-King's broken servants - show signs of resonance when inquisitors are near. Their eyes flicker with a purple glow. I believe this to be a reaction to the masks themselves.

Hypothesis: The masks act as conduits-vessels through which the God-King projects his will. The featureless faces allow his sight to flow through, unhindered by mortal limitations.

Theory: The inquisitors are not truly autonomous. They are hollowed men, empty shells. The masks are the foci. Without them, they may lose connection to his power. If I could obtain a mask, and dissect its enchantments, I might find a method to weaken, or break his hold….

Ace departed briefly to fetch a zombie specimen for Vaunt’s experiments. Meanwhile, Ranger shared the notes, and the professor’s irritation turned to fascination when he realized Ranger could read his coded script. When Ranger revealed the golden mask itself, Vaunt’s expression shifted from intrigue to hunger. He insisted on keeping it, promising answers in return.

### The Teleportation

Moments later, the rune circle blazed to life again. Vaunt shouted something over the roar of unstable magic—and in a flash of light, the world twisted once more.

When the disorientation faded, the companions found themselves standing in a familiar chamber. Kaelthar recognized it instantly: the hidden safehouse beneath the Sleeping Dragon Inn.

For the first time in days, the air was calm. No screams. No fire. Only silence.

They rested at last, their bodies hardened by battle and their minds sharpened by survival. They had grown stronger—tempered by the fires of Brightmarch.

That night, for the first time since entering the crypt, the adventurers slept in peace.
